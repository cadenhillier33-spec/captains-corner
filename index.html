🌅 The Horizon Scan

The Correction Deepens: Last week marked the worst stretch for equities since late 2024. The S&P 500 broke below 5,800, the Nasdaq shed 2.5%, and the VIX exploded to 29.49 — levels not seen since the banking crisis. This isn't a healthy pullback; it's a repricing of risk as the market confronts sticky inflation, elevated valuations, and geopolitical uncertainty.

Tech Under Siege: The AI trade that carried markets in 2024 is showing cracks. NVDA's post-earnings weakness (-3% Friday) signals that even blowout results aren't enough when multiples compress. The semis are leading the decline, with software names following. We're seeing a classic rotation from growth to value, from tech to staples, from risk to safety.

Gold's Safe Haven Bid: While equities crumbled, gold surged to $2,915 — a new all-time high. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.32%, pressuring growth stocks but supporting the dollar. This is the classic "stagflation-lite" trade: own real assets, avoid duration, and stay defensive.

CPI Week: Wednesday's February CPI report is the main event. Consensus expects 3.1% year-over-year, but the whisper number is higher. A print above 3.3% could send yields to 4.5% and stocks into a tailspin. Below 3.0% might spark a relief rally, but the trend is clear: inflation is sticky, and the Fed is stuck.

🎪 What If?

Scenario 1 — CPI Shock: Wednesday's February CPI (consensus 3.1% YoY) could surprise higher given the PPI heat. Above 3.3% = yields spike, stocks tank, Fed cut expectations pushed to Q4. Below 3.0% = relief rally, but unlikely given recent data.

Scenario 2 — Tech Bounce: If NVDA finds support at $175 and semis stabilize, we could see a violent short-covering rally. But the trend is down — any bounce is likely a selling opportunity until valuations reset.

Scenario 3 — Gold Parabolic: Gold at $2,915 is extended. A pullback to $2,800 would be healthy. But if equities continue to crumble, $3,000 is in play. Miners (NEM, GOLD) offer leverage.

Scenario 4 — VIX Spike: At 29.49, VIX is elevated but not extreme (2024 highs were 65+). If CPI disappoints, we could see 35-40. That would mark a tradable bottom — but we're not there yet.
