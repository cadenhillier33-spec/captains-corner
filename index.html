Week of May 11 — 15, 2026

01

The Horizon Scan

Macro Intelligence & Institutional Flow

What's happening: The market opens the May 11–15 week at record altitude. The S&P 500 sits at 7,398.93 after a +2.75% weekly move, while volatility has firmed with the VIX at 18.14. The 10-year Treasury has eased to 4.36%, giving growth stocks room to breathe — but inflation data now has the wheel.

What it means: This is an inflation-and-consumer week. BLS has April CPI scheduled for Tuesday at 8:30 a.m. ET and PPI for Wednesday at 8:30 a.m. ET; Census has April retail sales scheduled for Thursday at 8:30 a.m. ET. In plain English: investors are about to find out whether prices are cooling enough for the rally to keep breathing, and whether consumers are still spending enough to support earnings.

Our view: Stay constructive, but tighten the leash. The tape is strong, especially in growth and AI-linked names, while crude and volatility are both higher enough to keep macro risk alive. We still prefer quality AI infrastructure, cybersecurity, and power/data-center exposure, but only with disciplined entry points after the run.

02

Deep Water Analysis

Inflation Reclaims the Helm

Why this matters: Last week proved investors are still willing to pay for growth. This week tells us whether inflation lets them keep doing it. Strong earnings help, but if CPI or PPI runs hot, the market will start worrying that the Fed has to stay restrictive for longer.

The bottom line: The rally can continue if inflation cools and retail sales hold up. That would support the soft-landing story: slower inflation, still-healthy demand, and no urgent reason for the Fed to tighten the screws. The risk is the opposite — sticky inflation plus stretched valuations.

The Contrarian Compass

"The question is not whether AI is real. The question is whether investors can keep paying premium prices for AI winners while the 10-year Treasury sits in the mid-4s. If inflation behaves, the answer is yes. If it does not, price discipline comes back fast."

🎯

What This Means for Your Portfolio

The Fed, Inflation, and Your Risk Budget

The simple version: When inflation looks sticky, interest rates tend to stay higher. Higher rates make future profits worth less today, which usually hurts expensive growth stocks first.

What we are watching this week:

Key Takeaways for Investors

• Tuesday CPI is the biggest macro catalyst of the week
• Wednesday PPI checks whether producer costs are cooling
• Thursday retail sales tests whether the consumer is still spending
• Jobless claims help confirm whether labor cooling remains gradual
• A calm VIX does not mean single-stock risk is gone

How to think about your portfolio: Own quality, not hype. Favor businesses with real cash flow, visible demand, and pricing power. Keep some cash ready, because inflation-driven pullbacks can create better entries than chasing after a strong week.

Three Ways This Week Could Play Out

This week is about inflation first and the consumer second. CPI hits Tuesday, PPI Wednesday, and retail sales Thursday. The market can keep climbing if inflation cools and spending holds up. It gets harder if prices stay sticky, oil keeps firming, or the consumer suddenly weakens.

Scenario A: Goldilocks Inflation
"CPI/PPI cool, retail sales stay decent"
Likely market reaction:
• Growth and AI infrastructure keep leading
• Treasury yields stabilize or drift lower
• Market breadth improves beyond mega-cap tech

Our take: Stay constructive and add selectively to quality names, but do not chase extended charts.
Scenario B: Inflation Spoils It
"CPI or PPI runs hot and rates jump"
Likely market reaction:
• 10-year yields push higher
• Expensive growth stocks pull back first
• Cash-flow quality and defensives hold up better

Our take: Tighten risk, avoid adding to extended winners, and let better entry points come to us.
Scenario C: Consumer Wobbles
"Retail sales disappoint and claims tick up"
Likely market reaction:
• Equities could fall even if yields ease
• Gold and defensive assets catch a bid
• Speculative small-caps lag badly

Our take: Protect gains, raise cash, and build a shopping list instead of forcing trades into weak tape.

Base case: Scenario A, but with bumps. The trend is constructive; the risk is that inflation data reminds investors rates still matter.
