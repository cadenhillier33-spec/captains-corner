Week of April 20 — 24, 2026

01

The Horizon Scan

Macro Intelligence & Institutional Flow

What's happening: This was the week the ceasefire delivered. The S&P 500 surged 4.5% to close at a fresh record 7,126.06 — its best week in a year and its best three-week run (+11.9%) in six years. The Nasdaq Composite closed at 24,450.81, notching its 13th consecutive daily gain — the longest winning streak since 1992. The Dow tacked on +1.96% Friday alone to 49,532.30. The Friday fireworks came when Iran's foreign minister Abbas Araghchi declared the Strait of Hormuz "completely open" to commercial shipping for the duration of the 10-day Israel-Lebanon ceasefire, and President Trump confirmed it on Truth Social. Oil collapsed: WTI crude plunged 11.2% Friday to $83.52 — down 12.6% for the week — the sharpest weekly drop since the pandemic. Bank earnings kicked off the quarter on a high note: Goldman Sachs beat on both lines ($17.55 EPS vs. $16.49 expected, revenue $17.23B vs. $16.97B), with profit up 19% year-over-year. Netflix was the notable exception, selling off on cautious forward guidance despite meeting the quarter.

What it means: The two-narrative tension that defined the prior week — ceasefire optimism versus CPI shock — resolved decisively in favor of the ceasefire. With the strait confirmed open and oil back in the low-$80s, the mechanical path for headline CPI to roll back over is now visible. The VIX closed at 17.94, its lowest level since before the Iran war began — a pre-conflict volatility regime. The Dollar Index slipped to 98.23, its third straight weekly decline, as safe-haven demand bled out. The 10-Year Treasury held near 4.32%, effectively flat on the week, as the market balanced "inflation is cooling" against "a more hawkish, divided Fed." Gold did not sell off on the risk-on rotation: it pushed higher to $4,867.92/oz, +0.8% for the week and its fourth consecutive weekly gain — the smart money still hedging the durability of a conditional 10-day truce. Bitcoin finished the week at $75,746.90, up roughly 5% on the week as risk appetite broadened.

Our view: A Nasdaq 13-day win streak with VIX at 17.94 and the S&P at an all-time high is, historically, a regime where the crowd is long and complacent. The setup into this week is loaded. Tesla reports Wednesday after the close — its first major Q1 report after a brutal prior-quarter revision — and the readout on EV deliveries, robotaxis, and 2026 guidance will set the tone for the growth side of the AI trade. Intel reports Monday. Tuesday brings the delayed March Retail Sales print and the Kevin Warsh Fed Chair confirmation hearing — the latter is an under-appreciated market event given how much of the rally depends on Fed policy expectations. Friday delivers the final University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment for April; the preliminary reading already hit an all-time low on Iran war and inflation fears, so a recovery signal would be meaningful. We remain cautiously constructive, but we are actively trimming into strength. At 7,126 the S&P is pricing in ceasefire extension, clean bank credit, cooperative earnings, and a Fed path that is by no means settled. That is a lot of good news baked in. Base case: sideways digestion with sector rotation. Upside trigger: Tesla beats and Warsh signals dovish. Downside trigger: ceasefire breaks before it can be extended.

Deep Water Analysis

Records at the Top: When the Rally Earns Its Credit

Why this matters: The week of April 13–17 is the one skeptics — including us — were watching for proof. The market didn't just hold the prior week's ceasefire rally, it extended it decisively. The S&P 500 finished at 7,126.06 on Friday, a fresh all-time closing high, up 4.5% on the week. The Nasdaq's 13-day win streak matched a 34-year record, closing at 24,450.81. The pivotal moment came Friday morning when Iran's foreign minister confirmed the Strait of Hormuz was "completely open" for the duration of the 10-day Israel-Lebanon truce — the condition that had been unresolved the week before. Oil cratered on the news: WTI crude dropped 11.2% in a single session to $83.52, for a cumulative weekly loss of 12.6%. Every piece of the ceasefire thesis that was hypothetical a week ago became real: the strait is open, oil is back in the low-$80s, and the mechanical path for headline inflation to moderate is now in place. Bank earnings cooperated. Goldman Sachs led off with a clean beat ($17.55 EPS versus $16.49 consensus; revenue $17.23B versus $16.97B; profit +19% year-over-year). JPMorgan's quarter landed in line with its typical beat pattern. Netflix was the lone disappointment, selling off on guidance rather than the quarter itself.

The bottom line: Every week, we ask: is the rally earning its price, or running on fumes? This week, it earned it. The ceasefire held. The strait opened. Oil fell. Banks delivered. The VIX closed at 17.94 — a pre-war level — which tells you volatility sellers are being rewarded and the options market is pricing confidence. That said, records set on the back of a 10-day truce are not the same as records set on expanding fundamentals. The truce expiration clock is already ticking, and the May CPI print (May 12) will need to confirm that the energy-driven inflation shock is rolling over in real data, not just in futures curves. The Fed remains divided. FOMC minutes released earlier in April showed seven officials expecting just one cut in 2026 and seven more expecting none — a committee actively debating rate increases, not just holds. With the DXY down three straight weeks and gold at $4,867/oz (a fourth consecutive weekly gain), institutional positioning still has a real hedge on. That's the tension: index at a record, hedges at a record. Those two things usually don't coexist for long.

The Contrarian Compass

"A Nasdaq 13-day win streak, the S&P at an all-time high, the VIX at 17.94, and a ceasefire that expires in 10 days. Price that out. This is the setup where the crowd stops worrying and the smart money starts trimming. Three things tell us positioning is stretched: first, the Dollar Index fell a third straight week to 98.23 — that's not a risk-on tell, that's a capital-reallocation tell. Foreign money is trimming exposure to U.S. dollars at the same time U.S. equities hit records. Second, gold refused to sell off on the ceasefire; it actually gained 0.8% for the fourth consecutive weekly rise. When stocks make new highs and gold makes new highs simultaneously, the institutional hedge is on. Third, the University of Michigan preliminary sentiment for April just hit an all-time low on Iran war and price fears — the real-economy consumer is nowhere near as optimistic as the options market. The contrarian play this week is not to fade the rally, but to prepare for the hand-off. Tesla reports Wednesday after close — a miss or soft guide is the single most likely catalyst for a near-term pullback given how much the 13-day Nasdaq streak has been powered by mega-cap tech. Kevin Warsh's Fed Chair confirmation hearing Tuesday is the other wildcard no one is pricing correctly: if he signals a more hawkish path than Powell, rate-cut expectations reset and the 10-Year leaks higher. Buy insurance while it's this cheap. VIX at 17.94 is a gift."

How to think about your portfolio: Records are a double-edged signal. On the other edge, an all-time-high S&P at 7,126 paired with a 13-day Nasdaq streak and VIX at 17.94 is historically a regime where crowd positioning is long, options are cheap, and the next negative surprise moves the market harder than the next positive one. We are not calling a top — the trend is still up, and there is genuine evidence that the inflation shock is peaking. But at these levels, the marginal dollar works better as insurance than as exposure. Practical steps: review position sizing on your biggest winners from the three-week run, rebalance toward targets rather than letting winners ride unchecked, hold the gold and quality-bond hedges, and be mentally prepared to use any Tesla-miss or Warsh-hawkish-comment pullback as an opportunity to add quality at better prices. The barbell for this week: selectively long quality earnings beats + maintain hedges. Do not chase; let the market come to you.

Three Ways This Week Could Play Out

The week of April 20–24 is the second leg of earnings season — and it's the tech leg, the one that matters most for whether the Nasdaq's 13-day win streak can survive into a 14th, 15th, and beyond. Monday is quiet on the data side but Intel reports with focus on foundry progress and AI-chip demand. Tuesday is the week's pivot: the delayed March Retail Sales print lands at 8:30am (rescheduled from April 16), and the Kevin Warsh Fed Chair confirmation hearing begins — an event under-appreciated by markets given how much this rally depends on Fed-path expectations. Wednesday after the close delivers Tesla's Q1 — EV deliveries, robotaxi progress, energy storage, margins, and 2026 guidance all on the table. Thursday is the heaviest earnings day of the week with ~165 reports. Friday closes with the final University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment reading for April; the preliminary hit an all-time low, so any recovery would be meaningful.

Scenario A: Tesla Beats & Warsh Signals Continuity
"Tesla delivers a clean Q1 beat with constructive 2026 guidance, Warsh signals policy continuity in his confirmation testimony, March Retail Sales show the consumer holding up, and Michigan sentiment recovers off its all-time low."
Likely market reaction:
• S&P 500 extends record highs, testing 7,200 resistance
• Nasdaq streak pushes to 15+ days; mega-cap growth leads
• VIX compresses further toward 15–16; rate-cut odds re-firm for H2

Our take: This is the momentum bull case. The last piece of the puzzle for this rally is confirmation that growth demand is still intact and that the Fed's reaction function is known. Tesla is the single biggest near-term catalyst for growth sentiment, and Warsh's testimony will be parsed word-for-word. If both cooperate, the rally broadens beyond the ceasefire trade into genuine fundamental conviction. We would not add aggressively at these levels, but we would hold existing positions and use any midweek dip as an add opportunity in quality names.
Scenario B: Tesla Misses or Warsh Hawkish
"Tesla either misses on deliveries/margins or delivers soft 2026 guidance, or Warsh signals a more hawkish rate path than Powell. Either catalyst triggers the first real pullback in three weeks."
Likely market reaction:
• S&P pulls back to 6,950–7,000; the 13-day Nasdaq streak breaks
• VIX pops to 21–23 as stretched positioning unwinds
• Defensive rotation into staples, healthcare, and utilities

Our take: This is the most probable scenario, and it's not a bearish one — it's a healthy rest. At 7,126 on the S&P with VIX at 17.94, the market is priced for things to go right. Any single disappointment in mega-cap tech or hawkish Fed signal will produce a rotation, not a crash. We have been trimming into strength and would use a 2-3% pullback to re-add quality at better levels. This is the scenario we are positioned for.
Scenario C: Ceasefire Extension Stumbles
"The 10-day Israel-Lebanon truce approaches expiration without a clean extension framework, strait access becomes conditional again, or a geopolitical incident reminds the market the peace is contingent."
Likely market reaction:
• S&P drops to 6,800–6,900; oil gaps back above $90
• VIX spikes to 24+; gold breaks to new record highs
• The Nasdaq streak ends abruptly; three-week rally gives back half

Our take: This is the tail-risk scenario — not base case but meaningfully more probable than VIX at 17.94 suggests. The 10-day truce is a short fuse, and any slippage on extension talks before week's end would re-introduce the full energy-shock-plus-inflation narrative the market just successfully priced out. This is why gold remains in the book at +0.8% on the week despite a risk-on rotation: institutional money is hedging exactly this outcome. We maintain the hedge.

Base case: Scenario B — a digestion week with sector rotation as stretched mega-cap positioning cools. Tesla Wednesday evening is the single biggest near-term catalyst; Warsh Tuesday is the underappreciated one. Records at the top of a 13-day streak with VIX at 17.94 and a 10-day ceasefire clock running — the honest read is that the market is priced for perfection. We take the rally, protect the hedges, and let the week come to us.

CCL
Carnival Corporation
🚢 Oil Collapse Beneficiary

The ceasefire reopening that was only partial a week ago is now fully in effect for the duration of the 10-day truce. That creates operating leverage through the rest of the quarter if the ceasefire extends. The risk remains binary: any slippage on a truce extension, strait access becoming conditional again, or a geopolitical escalation re-prices the name quickly. This is a cleaner proxy for "does the ceasefire extend?" than betting directly on oil futures.

#3
GLD
SPDR Gold Shares ETF
🥇 Hedges Still On at Records

Gold finished the week at $4,867.92/oz, up 0.8% — its fourth consecutive weekly gain — even as equities made all-time highs and the VIX collapsed to 17.94. That combination is historically unusual: when stocks are setting records and volatility is compressing, gold typically sells off as the safe-haven bid fades. The fact that it hasn't is the single clearest signal that institutional positioning is hedging, not capitulating to the risk-on narrative. The Dollar Index falling a third straight week to 98.23 is the same story from a different angle — foreign capital is reducing dollar exposure. As long as the 10-day ceasefire clock is ticking and the Fed remains openly divided, gold has a reason to stay bid. It is the one book entry that does not care whether Tesla beats, Warsh is dovish, or the truce extends.
