Week of April 7 — 11, 2026

01

The Horizon Scan

Macro Intelligence & Institutional Flow

What's happening: The five-week losing streak is over. The S&P 500 surged 3.4% for the holiday-shortened week ending April 2 (markets closed Good Friday), closing at 6,582.69. The Nasdaq ripped 4.4% higher to 21,879.18, and the Dow added nearly 3.0% to finish at 46,504.67—pulling out of correction territory. The catalyst was a March jobs report that threaded the needle: nonfarm payrolls came in at +178,000 versus just 59,000 expected, nearly tripling consensus, while the unemployment rate dipped to 4.3% and average hourly earnings rose just 0.2% for the month (+3.5% YoY, the lowest since May 2021). The combination of resilient hiring with cooling wage growth was the Goldilocks outcome the market desperately needed. The week also marked the one-year anniversary of Trump's "Liberation Day" tariffs—largely struck down by the Supreme Court in February—with global investors using the milestone to rethink American asset allocations.

What it means: The jobs report changed the calculus on two fronts. First, the economy is not cracking under the weight of $120 oil and the Iran war—178K jobs added says the labor market is holding, even as the healthcare sector drove 76K of those gains (roughly 35K from returning strike workers, a one-time boost). Second, the wage deceleration to 3.5% YoY is the first meaningful sign that inflation pressures may be peaking outside of energy. The 10-Year Treasury yield fell to 4.31%, pricing in modestly higher rate-cut probability, while the VIX retreated to 24.54—still elevated, but a major improvement from last week's 31.05 panic reading. But the Iran war remains the overhang: on April 2, Trump warned he would strike Iran "extremely hard" and the conflict could last weeks, lifting oil prices and capping the rally's upside. The Strait of Hormuz disruption continues to threaten 20% of global oil supply.

Our view: One strong week does not make a trend reversal, but the breadth of this bounce—technology leading, VIX compressing, Treasury yields easing—has the hallmarks of something more than a dead-cat bounce. Gold pulled back to $4,675/oz (from $4,524) as the safe-haven bid softened slightly, while Bitcoin traded near $66,246, still struggling to reclaim $70K. The critical question for the week ahead: can the market hold these gains through Wednesday's FOMC minutes and Friday's CPI print? The minutes will reveal how the Fed is weighing the energy inflation shock against softening wages. CPI on Friday is the week's marquee event—if headline inflation shows signs of peaking, the rally extends. If energy costs are already filtering into core, the five-week selloff resumes. We moved from outright bearish to cautiously neutral. The defensive barbell (energy + short-duration) remains the core posture, but the risk-reward for selective quality names has improved materially.

02

Deep Water Analysis

The Snapback: Relief Rally or Regime Change?

Why this matters: After five consecutive weekly losses, the S&P 500's 3.4% weekly surge deserves serious analysis—not celebration. The index clawed back to 6,582.69 from the 6,368 low, but remains roughly 5.7% below the January record of 6,978. The Nasdaq's 4.4% gain was the strongest weekly performance in months, led by the very names that had been crushed: tech and growth getting their first meaningful bid since early March. The March jobs report was the spark—178K jobs on a 59K consensus, unemployment ticking down to 4.3%, wages cooling to 3.5% YoY. But strip out the 35K healthcare strike workers returning to payrolls, and the underlying gain is closer to 143K—solid, not spectacular. The one-year anniversary of Liberation Day tariffs provided a broader narrative backdrop: the Supreme Court struck most of them down in February, the government refunded $166B against $151B collected, and global investors used the milestone to publicly question "American exceptionalism." The Shanghai Composite, Korea's Kospi, and Japan's Nikkei 225 have all outperformed the three major U.S. indices since April 2, 2025. That's a structural rotation story that one good week doesn't reverse.

The bottom line: The week of March 31 – April 2 told us something important: this market is coiled, not broken. The speed of the rally—S&P +3.4%, Nasdaq +4.4%—shows massive short covering and sidelined cash ready to deploy at the first sign of good news. But the structural headwinds haven't changed. The Iran war entered its 34th day on April 2 with Trump escalating rhetoric about striking Iran "extremely hard." Oil remains elevated with the Strait of Hormuz disruption threatening 20% of global supply. The U.S. manufacturing sector has shed 100K jobs since the tariffs began. And the OECD's 4.2% inflation forecast for 2026 still looms over any Fed rate-cut timeline. The key distinction: last week's bounce was driven by data (cooling wages, resilient jobs), not by a resolution of any macro headwind. Data-driven rallies can sustain if the data continues to cooperate. Headline-driven rallies (like the March 23 ceasefire pop) evaporate overnight. This week's FOMC minutes (Wednesday) and CPI (Friday) will determine which category this bounce falls into.

The Contrarian Compass

"Everyone is celebrating the snapback—S&P +3.4%, Nasdaq +4.4%, VIX back to 24—and declaring the correction over. That's the contrarian setup in reverse: be cautious when others are relieved. The jobs report was genuinely good, but 35K of those gains were healthcare strike workers returning—a one-time mechanical boost that won't repeat. Wages at 3.5% YoY are cooling, but the OECD still projects 4.2% inflation and the Iran war is in its second month with no resolution. The real contrarian observation: international markets have outperformed the U.S. since Liberation Day one year ago. The 'American exceptionalism' trade that dominated 2023-2025 is being quietly unwound by institutional money. That doesn't mean sell everything—it means the reflexive assumption that every dip is a buying opportunity for U.S. mega-cap tech may no longer hold. The smartest money right now is diversifying geographically, not just adding to the same names on every pullback. Keep the energy overweight, keep the quality filter tight, and don't mistake a relief rally for an all-clear signal."

Three Ways This Week Could Play Out

The week of April 7–11 is a gauntlet of inflation data. Monday opens with thin volumes (Easter Monday closures in London and Hong Kong). Wednesday delivers FOMC minutes from the March meeting—the market will parse every word for how the Fed is weighing the energy inflation shock against cooling wages. Thursday brings the PCE price index. Friday is the main event: CPI, plus Michigan Consumer Sentiment and China's CPI. After last week's 3.4% S&P rally, the question is simple: can the data sustain the snapback, or does inflation reality reassert itself?

Scenario A: CPI Confirms the Cool-Down
"Headline CPI flat or declining, core shows deceleration — the wage-cooling story extends to prices. Rate-cut expectations surge."
Likely market reaction:
• S&P 500 extends rally toward 6,700+, potentially retesting January highs
• 10-Year Treasury yield drops toward 4.15–4.20%; rate-cut odds for summer jump
• Tech and growth lead; VIX compresses toward 20

Our take: This is the bull case. If CPI confirms what wages already suggested—that the inflation impulse is peaking—then the Fed has room to signal a summer cut. Combined with last week's strong jobs data, it paints a soft landing picture. We'd add to quality tech and reduce the energy overweight modestly. The five-week correction becomes a textbook buying opportunity in hindsight.
Scenario B: Energy Pass-Through Shows Up in CPI
"Headline CPI re-accelerates on energy, core sticky above expectations — the Strait of Hormuz premium is now in consumer prices."
Likely market reaction:
• Last week's rally reverses; S&P tests 6,400 support
• VIX spikes back toward 28–30; Treasury yields climb
• Energy resumes leadership; tech gives back gains

Our take: This is the bear case and the most dangerous outcome. It would confirm that the Iran war's oil disruption is filtering into core inflation—exactly what the OECD's 4.2% forecast predicted. The Fed cannot cut into rising core CPI. We revert to full defensive posture: energy, short-duration, cash. Last week's bounce becomes a bear-market rally.
Scenario C: Mixed Signals — FOMC Minutes Dominate
"CPI is roughly in-line, but FOMC minutes reveal deep division over the inflation outlook — the Fed is as uncertain as the market."
Likely market reaction:
• S&P 500 churns between 6,500–6,600; no clear direction
• VIX stays in the 22–26 range; directionless but not panic
• Sector rotation continues: value over growth, international over domestic

Our take: The muddle-through scenario. Markets hold last week's gains but don't extend them. The real catalyst gets pushed to Q1 earnings season and the next FOMC meeting. We maintain the cautiously neutral posture—barbell intact, selectively adding quality on pullbacks, but not chasing the rally higher without a clear inflation signal.

We're positioned for Scenario B or C — cautiously neutral with the defensive barbell intact. Friday's CPI is the week's singular event, but Wednesday's FOMC minutes could move markets if the committee reveals more division than expected. Easter Monday thin volumes could amplify early-week moves in either direction. PCE on Thursday is the warm-up act for CPI.
